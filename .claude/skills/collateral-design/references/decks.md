# Decks, Presentations & Self-Contained Sheets

> **A deck is a digital medium — treat it like a screen, not a print piece.**

The deck is the collateral format most often built wrong, because it *looks* easy — a title box and a bullet placeholder on every slide.

## Decide the Mode First: Presented or Read

Before a single slide, answer: **will a person present this live, or will it be sent and read alone?**

- **Presented (talk-track).** The presenter carries the words. Slides are **sparse** — one idea, a big visual, a few words of anchor.
- **Read (leave-behind / send).** No narrator. Every slide must **stand on its own** — the point, the support, and enough context to be understood cold.

If the deck must do both, the honest answer is usually **two versions or a hybrid**.

## The Deck Is a Sequence, Not a Stack

A deck has an **arc**. Each slide earns its place in a narrative:

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide has two ideas, it's two slides.
- **Rhythm across the deck.** A shared master holds the deck together; **varied layouts** keep it alive.
- **Section dividers signal structure.**

## Slide Anatomy

- **A master, not a per-slide guess.** Fixed margins, a safe title zone, a consistent footer.
- **Hierarchy per slide.** Name the one thing that matters on this slide.
- **Kill bullet soup.** Bullets are the enemy of hierarchy.
- **Type scaled for the room.**
- **Contrast for the projector.**

## Data Slides

- **One takeaway, and the takeaway is the title.** Not "Q3 Revenue" — "Revenue doubled after launch."
- **Style the chart to the system.**
- **Reduce the chart to its point.**

## Self-Contained Sheets (One-Pagers, Sales Sheets, Leave-Behinds)

A one-pager is a *read* artifact with **no scroll and no presenter**:

- **One primary message, ranked proof beneath it.**
- **A clear entry point and reading path.**
- **Balance text with a visual.**
- **It stands alone.**

## Common Deck Failures

- Every slide a title + three bullets
- A read-deck built as one-word talk-track slides
- Data slides titled with the metric instead of the takeaway
- Twenty identical layouts
- Under-scaled type and low-contrast pairs
- The logo huge on every slide
